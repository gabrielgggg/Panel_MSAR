\section{Setup}

The fitted trend is linear in calendar time (common $g$, country intercept $a_i$).
A cliff in the plotted cycle is therefore a cliff in log real GDP per worker.
Consecutive-quarter changes with $|\Delta y|\ge 0.25$ (about 28\% QoQ) were
flagged and split into local GDP, USD GDP, employment, and the base-year
exchange rate.

\section{Main finding}

Currency units are not the main story.
The base-year exchange rate is a country constant; its log-difference
is zero at every flagged jump.
Known redenominations (Romania 2005, Belarus 2016, and others) are smooth in
this file.
The cliffs on the cycle plot are almost entirely \textbf{employment scale or
source breaks}, plus one \textbf{GDP units break} (El Salvador, 2005).

\section{Distinct level shifts}

These are the one-time (or paired) cliffs, not repeating seasonality.
$\Delta\log y$ is the change in log real GDP per worker from the previous
quarter.

\section{Notes}

Slovak Republic and Israel are the same $4\times$ employment-unit bug already
patched in the SOE extract; it is back in this file.
Montenegro 2023 looks like a single annual number copied into every quarter.

NSA seasonality (Romania, Bulgaria, Montenegro, Mongolia, and others, with
$|\Delta y|\approx 0.25$--$0.35$ every winter) is a repeating pattern, not a
one-time cliff.

$|\Delta y|\ge 0.25$). Distinct events: jump-events.csv.
